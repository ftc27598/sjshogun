1. Welcome and challenge
HackShogun is a beginner-friendly virtual technology competition organized by SJ Shogun. Build a working solution to a meaningful problem that could make tomorrow better. Ideas can relate to health, education, accessibility, robotics, science, sustainability, safety or another issue. Websites, apps, AI tools, games with real-world purposes and hardware prototypes are welcome. No prior programming experience is necessary.
2. Eligibility and teams
Participants must be 13–18 during the event, register individually, join Discord, attend the mandatory opening ceremony and follow the event rules. Any required parent or guardian permissions must be completed before taking part.
All entries are team entries. Each team has 1–4 registered participants. A one-person team is fully eligible and competes in the same division as larger teams. Each person may join only one team. Each team builds one project and submits once. One team representative is the captain and primary organizer contact. The team confirmation deadline will be announced in Discord. Changes after that deadline need organizer approval.
3. Registration and participation
Each participant completes the registration Google Form. Register separately even if your team is already formed.
Join the official Discord and follow announcements, workshop information and technical updates.
Confirm your team roster using the process announced by organizers. Solo participants form one-person teams.
Attend the opening ceremony on Friday, January 15 at 5:00 PM EST. Building begins afterward, at approximately 5:30 PM.
Build your project during the event and have one representative submit the project Google Form by Sunday, January 17 at 5:00 PM EST.
4. Event schedule
Friday, January 15, 5:00 PM EST: Mandatory opening ceremony on Discord, including rules, judging, GitHub instructions, FIRST Robotics introduction and Q&A. Building begins after the ceremony at approximately 5:30 PM.
Saturday, January 16: Main development day. Optional mentoring, coding and AI workshops, GitHub help and robotics programming may be available. Confirmed times will appear in Discord.
Sunday, January 17, 5:00 PM EST: Final submission deadline and code freeze. Finish testing, your README, video, written responses and link checks before the final hour.
5. Original work and team collaboration
Most project-specific development must happen during HackShogun. Planning and learning beforehand are allowed, but substantially completed projects from school, past hackathons, portfolios or other events may not be entered unchanged. Existing substantial code must be disclosed.
You may use libraries, templates, starter code, public APIs, datasets, documentation and other open-source resources. Disclose major external components. Teammates may write and review each other’s code. Mentors and members of other teams can provide general advice, but must not create significant project features on your behalf. Copying another competing team’s work is prohibited.
6. AI tools
AI coding, writing, design and debugging tools are allowed. They do not automatically lower your score. Explain meaningful AI assistance and verify all generated work. Every team should be able to explain its architecture, important features, development challenges and member contributions. Judges may ask technical questions or review unclear authorship.
7. GitHub and repository
Use one repository per team. Commit and push regularly. Include source code, important assets, installation or run instructions, dependencies and a useful README. Never publish secret API keys, passwords or other credentials. The repository must be public, and judges must be able to access your final repository. After the deadline, do not materially change your submitted work unless organizers authorize an access correction.
8. Final project submission
Only one team representative submits the official project Google Form. This is not the participant registration form. The deadline is Sunday, January 17 at 5:00 PM EST.
Team name, captain contact and names plus registered emails of all members
Project name and brief summary
Accessible GitHub repository URL
Accessible 3–5 minute demonstration video URL
Four written answers shown below
Disclosure of AI tools, external resources and meaningful pre-existing code
Confirmation of original work and compliance with the rules
Written responses
Mission & Problem, up to 400 words: What problem do you solve, why does it matter and who needs your solution?
Solution & User Experience, up to 600 words: What did you build and how does it work for a user?
Technical Architecture, up to 600 words: What languages, libraries, APIs and systems are involved? Explain one technical obstacle and your response.
Innovation & Future Scope, up to 400 words: What makes your approach useful or different, what are its limitations and what would you add next?
Demonstrate the actual working project in your video, not just slides. The judges will not be required to watch beyond five minutes. Google Drive, YouTube or comparable viewable links are acceptable if access does not require an approval request. Test video and repository links in a signed-out browser window. Draft written responses separately before pasting them into the form. Submit before the deadline.
9. Judging and finalists
Judges score one project per team across Innovation & Creativity, Technical Execution, Functionality & Execution, Presentation & Communication and Impact & Usefulness. A clear, functional prototype can outperform a more ambitious but incomplete project. The same criteria apply to one-person and multi-person teams.
Approximately nine highest-scoring submissions may advance to a finalist round, subject to participation and judging logistics. Finalists may be invited to demonstrate live, explain design decisions and answer questions about their code, AI usage and individual contributions. Updates will be announced through official channels.
10. Conduct, safety and issues
Respect everyone. Harassment, bullying, plagiarism, impersonation, disruption, sabotage, malicious submissions and deliberate rule violations can result in removal or disqualification. Do not publicly post private contact details, addresses, school credentials, financial details, passwords or confidential API keys. Use official Discord channels for event matters when possible.
A suspicious commit pattern or inaccessible link may prompt review but is not automatic misconduct. Ordinary internet failures, late uploads and forgotten submissions do not normally justify an extension. Organizers may provide fair exceptions for widespread outages of official event platforms and will announce changes in Discord.
